# Expected Output Rubric

This rubric defines what strong governance skill output should include.

## 1. Decision Quality

Strong outputs should clearly state the decision, status, or recommendation.

Examples:

- Not ready for CAB.
- Approve with conditions.
- Hold vendor approval.
- Do not submit evidence as-is.
- Residual risk remains high.
- AI use case not approved for production.

## 2. Governance Gap Detection

Strong outputs should identify gaps such as:

- Missing approvals.
- Weak validation evidence.
- Incomplete rollback plans.
- Missing monitoring evidence.
- Pending security or compliance review.
- Undefined owners.
- Missing due dates.
- Unclear scope or business justification.
- Missing audit traceability.

## 3. Evidence Requirements

Strong outputs should identify required evidence, including:

- Approval records.
- Test results.
- Validation screenshots.
- Monitoring logs.
- RCA links.
- Risk register entries.
- Security review disposition.
- DPA or SOC 2 evidence.
- AI terms and model training restrictions.

## 4. Risk Framing

Strong outputs should explain risk in practical terms, such as:

- Operational disruption.
- Audit finding exposure.
- Customer or business impact.
- Delayed detection.
- Data exposure.
- Vendor dependency.
- Weak control operation.

## 5. Actionability

Strong outputs should include next steps, owners, or remediation actions.

Examples:

- Assign named owner.
- Confirm approval timing.
- Attach validation evidence.
- Document rollback steps.
- Hold submission until gaps are resolved.
- Use synthetic data for pilot.

## 6. Professional Tone

Strong outputs should be direct, objective, and governance-focused. They should avoid blame and focus on evidence, risk, accountability, and decision readiness.
